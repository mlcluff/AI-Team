# BYU-Idaho Academic Office Manager — Initial Research Package

**Prepared by:** **Research**  
**Date:** 2026-10-07  
**Status:** Preliminary; validate with current academic office managers before treating as the final knowledge base

## Research question

What resources, systems, and contacts are needed in the daily role of an academic office manager at BYU-Idaho, and how can the knowledge be organized into a one-hour scenario-based e-learning module with three formative assessments and one summative assessment?

## Initial conclusion

The role appears to depend on four daily capability areas: (1) triaging requests and routing them to the correct office, (2) using institutional systems and service channels, (3) protecting student and employee information while documenting work, and (4) coordinating schedules, people, forms, and follow-up. The knowledge base should be organized around realistic office scenarios rather than a directory of disconnected facts.

## Priority scenarios for the e-learning module

1. A student or faculty member asks a question that could belong to Advising, Registration, Financial Aid, or another office. The learner must clarify the request and route it correctly.
2. A staff member reports a technology or access problem. The learner must select the correct IT channel and capture the information needed for a useful ticket.
3. A request involves a hold, record, grade, financial information, or another sensitive matter. The learner must avoid overstepping, protect information, and escalate to the authorized office.
4. A deadline, closure, or unavailable contact affects service. The learner must check current hours and provide a realistic next step.

## Draft one-hour learning architecture

This is a design hypothesis for validation, not the final instructional design.

- 0–8 minutes: role context, service mindset, information boundaries, and navigation of the knowledge base.
- 8–20 minutes: resources and systems map; Formative Assessment 1 — identify the correct destination for common requests.
- 20–35 minutes: contact lookup, hours, and escalation; Formative Assessment 2 — choose the best contact and channel.
- 35–48 minutes: documentation, privacy, and follow-up; Formative Assessment 3 — identify what to record and what not to disclose.
- 48–60 minutes: integrated scenario-based summative assessment requiring triage, system selection, contact selection, documentation, and escalation.

## Elite standard for this research

The final package must be validated with current practitioners and official institutional owners, distinguish role-specific practice from generic office administration, document system permissions and privacy boundaries, and include observable performance criteria. A list of links alone is insufficient.

## Gaps requiring practitioner validation

- Exact academic office manager duties, authority, and service-level expectations by college or department.
- Current systems used for scheduling, room reservations, purchasing, travel, forms, personnel actions, and academic workflows.
- What academic office managers may access or change in each system.
- Escalation paths for student privacy, academic exceptions, emergencies, and complaints.
- Common high-frequency and high-consequence scenarios.
- Best contact names and backup contacts for each academic unit.
- WGU capstone constraints, required instructional-design framework, and assessment requirements.
